## 1. Business Model & Positioning

### What Algoryxz should be at launch
**[REC]** Start as a **productized service studio**, not a pure freelance agency and not a software platform. Concretely: a small menu of fixed-scope packages (not "custom quote for everything") delivered fast using your AI-agent workflow, with an *optional* subscription tier layered on top. This is a hybrid, but weighted toward "studio" until you have ~10 recurring clients — at that point the internal platform (Phase 4) starts to look justified as a genuine product, not before.

Why not a pure agency: three college students without a track record can't win on "we have a proven process and 15 years of experience" — that's an agency's pitch. Why not "custom quote for everything": open-ended scoping burns your scarcest resource (time against coursework), and it makes every sale a negotiation instead of a click-to-buy.

### Best initial customer segments
**[REC]** Two tiers, deliberately different in economics:
1. **Personal/emotional-occasion sites** (weddings, proposals, anniversaries, birthdays) — fast to build, word-of-mouth-driven, low technical risk, good margin per hour, and an easy way to get your first 10–20 paying customers through your own social/college network. This is your **wedge**, not your destination.
2. **Local small businesses** (cafés, salons, gyms, photographers, shops) — lower margin per project but the only segment that can plausibly generate **recurring revenue** (hosting/maintenance retainers), which is what actually stabilizes the business.

Don't chase "college club" or "student business" work as a paid category — treat it as free/heavily discounted portfolio-building, not revenue.

### Should occasion sites and business sites share one brand?
**[REC] — this is a place to push back on the concept.** Combining "we build your restaurant's website" and "we build your wedding proposal site" under one brand is workable *only if the site (Algoryxz) frames itself around the underlying capability* — fast, well-crafted, AI-accelerated web builds — rather than trying to look like a wedding studio to wedding clients and a serious B2B vendor to business clients simultaneously. The failure mode: a business owner researching you lands on a portfolio full of wedding invites and proposal pages and doesn't take you seriously for their storefront/booking system, or vice versa.
**[REC]** Mitigate with clear **portfolio segmentation on the website itself** (e.g., "Business & Brands" vs "Celebrations & Moments" as separate portfolio tracks under one studio brand, one visual identity, one process page). Don't run them as two brands — that doubles your marketing and legal overhead for no real benefit at this stage.

### Differentiation vs freelancers, local agencies, Wix/Framer/Webflow/AI builders
**[REC]** Be explicit with yourselves about what "AI agents" actually buys you, because it is *not* a customer-facing differentiator (per your own brief) — it's an internal speed/cost lever. Your real differentiation has to be human-facing:
- **Speed at a fixed, credible price** — because AI-assisted build genuinely lets three students turn around a quality site faster than a solo freelancer, you can commit to timelines a freelancer can't.
- **Design + technical range in one team** — most solo freelancers are either designer-who-codes-badly or developer-who-designs-badly. You have Deeptiman (design) and Smarak (engineering) as separate strong people, which a ₹15–50k freelancer usually isn't.
- **Custom booking/DB/auth work when needed** — Wix/Framer/no-code builders cap out fast once a client needs real backend logic (bookings, dashboards, payments); that's your technical ceiling advantage.
- **A studio that answers, iterates, and maintains** — vs. DIY builders where the business owner is on their own after signup.

What you will *not* win on at launch: price-floor competition against Fiverr/commodity freelancers (₹3,000–8,000 sites), or brand trust against 5+ year agencies for large corporate clients. Don't compete there.

### How the business should evolve if it succeeds
**[REC]** Realistic staged path:
1. **Stage 0 (now–6 months):** Sole-prop-adjacent operation under one founder's PAN (see Section 2), productized packages, portfolio built from real + concept projects, first 5–15 clients.
2. **Stage 1 (6–18 months, if traction is real):** Formalize as LLP or Pvt Ltd (Section 2), add the recurring maintenance tier, first paid retainers, consider whether the internal ops tool (Phase 4) is worth building vs. Notion+Linear.
3. **Stage 2 (18+ months, only if genuinely justified):** Either (a) stay a profitable boutique studio — a perfectly good outcome — or (b) if a repeatable *product* emerges from the work (e.g., the visual-feedback client-portal tooling, or a vertical template product for wedding sites), spin that out as the software platform. Don't try to be the studio and the platform company simultaneously before Stage 1 revenue is proven; that's the classic way three-person teams spread themselves too thin.

### Where the current concept is weak
**[REC], being direct as asked:**
- **Section 5/6 (the internal AI-agent orchestration platform) is over-scoped for where the business actually is.** You're describing something close to a mini Linear + mini GitHub Projects + mini agent-orchestration framework, before you have a single paying client. Build the *cheapest possible version* (a shared doc/board) first; the ambitious version is a Phase-2-of-success problem, not a launch requirement. This is the single biggest risk to your timeline and to actually shipping client work.
- **The "advanced systems" list (booking, auth, payments, CMS, automations) is client-facing scope creep risk** — without a clear "what's in the base package vs. what's a quoted add-on" line, you'll under-price complex builds by accident. Needs a hard rule from day one (Section 3).
- **"AI as internal infrastructure, not the pitch" is the right call** — confirmed by how AI-website-builder marketing (Wix ADI, Framer AI, etc.) is already commoditizing "AI website" as a phrase; leading with it would put you in a price-war category you can't win, not a premium one.

## 2. Three-Founder Legal & Business Foundation (India)

### Structures available
**[FACT]** India has no direct "LLC" equivalent; the two realistic options for three founders are a **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)** or a **Private Limited Company** — both filed through the MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) portal.
**[FACT]** All-in incorporation cost: LLP typically **₹5,000–₹15,000** (MCA fees ₹1,200–₹6,000 + DSC ₹800–₹1,500/partner + stamp duty, state-dependent); Private Limited typically **₹7,000–₹25,000** all-inclusive for a standard multi-director company, with stamp duty and professional fees as the main variable. SPICe+ filing itself is free for authorised capital up to ₹15 lakh.
**[FACT]** Ongoing annual compliance is materially cheaper for an LLP (**~₹5,000–₹15,000/year**) than for a Pvt Ltd (**~₹15,000–₹40,000/year**, including statutory audit, ROC annual return, ITR, and director KYC).
**[FACT]** A Pvt Ltd can issue shares/ESOPs and is the standard structure for raising outside equity funding later; an LLP technically can raise from investors but this is rare in practice — most Indian VC/angel funding structurally expects a Pvt Ltd.

**[REC]** Given three founders, service-based revenue, no funding plans in year one, and a strong preference for low admin overhead while still in college: **start as an LLP once you're past the "are we actually getting paying clients" validation stage.** Don't incorporate anything on day zero.

### The earliest validation period, before incorporating anything
**[REC]** For your first handful of paying clients (proof of concept, before you know if this is real), operate informally: one founder invoices as an individual (sole proprietor under their own PAN, no formal registration required to start), and split proceeds between the three of you by private agreement. This is legally simplest but has real limits — see below.
**[PRO ADVICE]** This informal-split approach has real tax and liability exposure (the invoicing founder is personally on the hook for the full contract, and internally "splitting" money between founders isn't a recognized legal structure — it needs at minimum a written founder agreement, and ideally a CA's input on how to treat the transfers so they aren't misclassified). Don't run this for more than a few small projects before formalizing.

### Founder agreement — must cover regardless of entity type
**[REC]** Get this in writing before your first real client payment, even informally, even as a one-page doc between the three of you:
- Equity/profit split (need not be equal — reflect actual contribution)
- IP ownership (Algoryxz owns all client work product and internal tools, not any individual founder)
- What happens if one founder leaves (vesting-style thinking, even informally — e.g., "if you leave in year one you keep X%, not full equity")
- Decision-making (who breaks ties on the three technical/design/ops leads)
- Time commitment expectations while all three are still in college
**[PRO ADVICE]** A lawyer-drafted founder agreement (even a lightweight one) is worth the cost once you incorporate — verbal/handshake agreements between friends are the single most common cause of founder disputes later.

### GST
**[FACT]** GST registration is mandatory once turnover crosses **₹20 lakh/year for services** in normal-category states (Odisha is a normal-category state, so ₹20 lakh applies to you), calculated across all business activity under one PAN. Voluntary registration below that threshold is allowed.
**[REC]** Don't register for GST until you're near the threshold or a client specifically needs a GST invoice for their own input credit — early voluntary registration just adds filing overhead with no benefit at your scale.
**[PRO ADVICE]** Exact treatment of inter-state clients, exports (if you ever bill international clients), and the interaction with your chosen entity type should be confirmed with a CA.

### Udyam/MSME, PAN/TAN, banking, bookkeeping
**[FACT]** PAN and TAN are auto-allotted during SPICe+ incorporation for a Pvt Ltd at no extra cost; for an LLP, PAN/TAN are applied for post-incorporation.
**[REC]** Register on the **Udyam portal** (free, self-declaration based) once you have an entity — it's low-effort and unlocks MSME benefits (some government tenders, delayed-payment protection from clients, occasionally better loan terms) with no real downside.
**[REC]** Open a current account in the entity's name once incorporated (a founder's personal savings account should not be used for business revenue once the LLP/Pvt Ltd exists). Use **Zoho Books** for bookkeeping — genuinely free for turnover under ₹25 lakh/year (1 user + 1 accountant, GST invoicing, up to 1,000 transactions/year), which comfortably covers a pre-revenue-scale studio.
**[PRO ADVICE]** Income tax treatment (LLP profit share vs Pvt Ltd salary/dividend structuring), and whether/when to bring in a part-time CA for filings, needs professional input — don't self-file company/LLP returns.

### Contracts & invoicing
**[REC]** Every paid project needs, at minimum: a one-page scope document, a payment schedule (see Section 4), and an invoice with your entity's details once incorporated. Use Zoho Invoice/Books for this rather than building anything — see Section 4/20 territory.

## 3. Pricing & Economics

### Market anchors (verified ranges, India, 2026)
**[FACT]** General small-business website market: freelancers **₹15,000–₹50,000+**; a "cheap commodity" layer exists at ₹3,000–15,000 (largely Fiverr-style/gig-marketplace competition, race-to-the-bottom, not where you should compete); boutique agencies **₹50,000–₹3,00,000+** for the same scope with process/QA/multi-specialist teams; hourly freelance rates **₹400–₹2,000/hour** depending on experience.
**[FACT]** Tier-2 India small businesses (Bhubaneswar fits this) commonly expect to pay **₹5,000–₹15,000** for a basic site from a local freelancer/studio, vs. ₹50,000–₹2,00,000 that Tier-1 metro startups are comfortable paying for similar scope. This gap is real and matters for your local pricing.
**[FACT]** Existing templated wedding-website products already sell at **₹999–₹4,999** (bundled RSVP/gallery/hosting, e.g. "SitesPlaced"), while semi-custom studio-built wedding sites run **₹4,999–₹19,999+** depending on design depth (per Rachnakar Designs' published tiers: entry ~₹4,999, "Premium" ~₹9,999, "Luxe" ~₹19,999+).

### Recommended Algoryxz pricing (introductory vs. sustainable)
**[REC]** These are starting anchors — adjust after your first 5 real projects tell you your actual hours-per-project:

|---|---|---|---|
| Proposal / single-page occasion site | ₹1,500–₹3,000 | ₹3,000–₹6,000 | 1–2 day turnaround, template-adjacent but custom-designed |

**[REC]** Don't go below the "introductory" floor above even for friends/college network — you can *discount* explicitly (e.g., "50% college-friend rate") but keep the sticker price real, so your portfolio's public pricing page stays credible for actual clients.

## 4. Recurring Revenue

### Structure
**[REC]** One-time build fee + optional monthly plan, not "unlimited changes" at any tier — the search evidence and general SaaS/agency practice both support this: unlimited-scope retainers are the most common way small studios silently go underwater, because support burden is uncapped while the fee isn't.

**[REC] Suggested plan tiers:**

| Plan | Price/mo (introductory) | Includes |
|---|---|---|
| **Care** (hosting+uptime only) | ₹499–₹999 | Hosting, SSL, uptime monitoring, security patching, 1 content tweak/month |
| **Grow** (maintenance + small changes) | ₹1,500–₹3,000 | Everything in Care + up to 2 hrs dev time/month, monthly backup, basic analytics review |
| **Scale** (active partner) | ₹4,000–₹8,000+ | Everything in Grow + priority turnaround, small landing pages/SEO content, quarterly strategy check-in |

**[REC]** Cancellation: no lock-in below 3 months minimum commitment (gives you predictable near-term revenue without trapping small clients who genuinely can't afford it long-term); on cancellation, client gets a clean handover per Section 14, not held hostage.

## 5. Founder Operating Model

**[REC]** Primary ownership, keeping your stated tendencies but adding explicit secondary coverage so no single founder is a bottleneck:

- **Smarak** — primary: architecture, backend/infra, AI-agent tooling, deployments. Secondary: technical scoping on client calls (translating what a client wants into what's feasible/priced correctly).
- **Deeptiman** — primary: UI/UX, visual design, brand identity, portfolio site design. Secondary: client-facing presentation of design work (walkthroughs, revision conversations — clients respond well to the person who made the thing explaining it).
- **Akriti** — primary: QA, research (competitor/market research for each new project type), ops/organization. Secondary: **client relationship ownership** — see below.

**[REC]** **Client ownership should be per-project, assigned to whoever is closest to that client's segment, not rotating randomly** — e.g., Akriti or Deeptiman likely owns most wedding/personal-occasion clients (softer, relationship-heavy communication), Smarak likely owns technically complex builds (booking systems, custom apps) where scoping conversations are technical. One named owner per client avoids the "three people editing everything" problem you flagged.

**[REC]** Approval gates: Deeptiman signs off design before dev starts; Smarak signs off technical feasibility/architecture before a quote goes out for anything beyond a template site; Akriti signs off QA before anything ships to a client. No single founder ships solo past these gates, even under time pressure — this is cheap insurance against the exact chaos Section 8 of your brief is worried about, achievable with a shared checklist, no tooling required yet.

**[REC]** Money handling, operationally (not deciding equity — that's your call): one shared ledger (a spreadsheet is genuinely fine at this scale) logging every client payment in/expense out, reconciled monthly, visible to all three. Don't let cash sit in one founder's personal account "to be sorted later" — that's how disputes start.

**[REC]** Uneven contribution on a specific project: track it (even informally, e.g., a shared hours log), and let it inform the *next* project's split or a small per-project bonus, rather than trying to solve it via base equity — equity should reflect overall commitment, not any single project's hours.

## 7. Pre-mortem: most likely reasons Algoryxz fails in year one

**[REC]** Ranked by how directly they threaten *your specific* situation (three college students, ambitious platform vision, AI-agent workflow):

1. **You build Phase 4 (the internal agent-orchestration platform) before you have paying clients, and burn your limited hours on internal tooling instead of client work or coursework.** This is the highest-probability failure mode given how detailed and ambitious that part of your brief already is. Mitigation: hard rule — no work on the internal platform until you have 5 paying clients *and* a concrete pain point a spreadsheet/Notion genuinely can't solve.
2. **College workload collapses available hours during exams/semester crunch, client work slips, reputation damage locally (small-town/college network word-of-mouth cuts both ways).** Mitigation: be explicit with clients up front about turnaround windows around exam periods; don't overcommit calendar capacity.
3. **Underpriced early work becomes the expected price** — friends/college network get used to ₹2,000 sites and refuse to pay ₹10,000 later, or new prospects find your old cheap work and anchor to it. Mitigation: keep a visible "introductory vs. standard" pricing distinction from day one (Section 3), and don't let free/heavily-discounted work appear in the public portfolio at full-price framing.
4. **Scope creep on "advanced systems" (booking, auth, payments, custom apps) eats margin because these were quoted like a website instead of custom software.** Mitigation: hard internal rule — anything beyond the fixed-package feature list gets scoped and quoted separately, never absorbed.
5. **No written founder agreement, and an uneven-contribution dispute (one founder does much more design/dev work than the others on a stretch of projects) causes a rift.** Mitigation: Section 5/2's agreement and hours-logging, done early, before money or resentment is on the table.
6. **Client data mishandling** (wedding guest lists, personal photos, payment info) without any privacy practice, causing a real trust/reputation incident in a small local market. Mitigation: baseline data-handling hygiene from project one — this is flagged again in the legal Phase (DPDP Act) but the *practice* should start now, not wait for that research.
